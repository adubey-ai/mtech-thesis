\chapter{Conclusions and Future Work}\label{ch:conclusion}

\section{Summary of Contributions}\label{sec:concl_summary}
The work described in this thesis developed and evaluated a
modular high-performance software stack for training and serving
Large Language Models at scale. The principal contributions are:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item A controlled comparison of four distributed training
          strategies on an 8-GPU server, complete with detailed
          measurements of GPU utilisation, memory, and per-epoch
          energy (Chapter~\ref{ch:distributed}). DeepSpeed ZeRO-3
          attained the highest peak GPU utilisation ($93.8\%$)
          and the lowest per-epoch energy ($22\%$ below DP), while
          FSDP matched ZeRO-3 on memory efficiency with a simpler
          deployment story.
    \item A treatment of CUTLASS and CuTe GEMM pipelines and of
          their mapping onto the dense operations of a transformer
          layer (Chapter~\ref{ch:cutlass}). CUTLASS matched cuBLAS
          on large square GEMMs and outperformed it by $35\%$ on
          skinny attention shapes and $31\%$ on grouped MoE
          shapes; epilogue fusion recovered an additional
          $10$--$25\%$ of end-to-end HBM traffic.
    \item A Sliding Window Flash Attention algorithm that combines
          block classification, vectorised mask generation, up to
          $65.6\%$ block skipping, and a closed-form optimal
          window derivation whose loss is strictly convex
          (Chapter~\ref{ch:swfa}). SWFA delivered a
          $50$--$59\%$ throughput improvement on GPT-2 Medium
          training and remained within $10^{-4}$ of the FP32
          reference across $19$ validation configurations.
    \item Kernel-level optimisations inside the SGLang serving
          runtime targeted at long-context inference
          (Chapter~\ref{ch:sglang}), with a cumulative
          $2.84\times$ decode-throughput speedup on LLaMA-7B at
          $w = 4096$ and a p99 latency below $30$\,ms at batch
          $64$.
    \item A Triton-based profiling and auto-tuning pipeline
          (Chapter~\ref{ch:triton}) that reproduced the CUTLASS
          numbers to within $3\%$ and therefore provides an
          independent cross-check on both the SWFA and SGLang
          measurements.
\end{enumerate}
Taken together, the end-to-end evaluation shows a
$50$--$59\%$ gain in throughput and a $25$--$29\%$ drop in
energy for a GPT-2 Medium workload when SWFA is paired with
either DeepSpeed or FSDP, which is evidence that the layers of
the stack genuinely compose rather than cancel one another.

\section{Lessons Learned}\label{sec:concl_lessons}

\subsection{Layering Wins Over Point Optimisation}\label{sec:concl_layering}
Not a single optimisation in this thesis delivers the
end-to-end result on its own. The $59\%$ training-throughput
gain shows up only when the SWFA kernel, the sharded training
schedule, and the epilogue-fused GEMMs are all in place
simultaneously. The same point reappears in the SGLang chapter
from a different angle: the $2.84\times$ serving speedup is the
product of four independent kernels, each of which clears a
different bottleneck.

\subsection{Block-Level Sparsity Is Practical}\label{sec:concl_sparsity}
Element-level sparsity is hard to translate into wall-clock gain
on a modern Tensor Core: the $16 \times 8 \times 16$ MMA
instructions dispatch whole dense tiles regardless of how many
individual elements are zero. Block-level sparsity fits the
hardware far more cleanly---each tile is either fully present or
fully absent. SWFA shows this at the granularity of the attention
matrix, and the same principle should carry over to other
structured sparsity families (strided, block-sparse,
BigBird-like).

\subsection{Profiling Is a First-Class
Deliverable}\label{sec:concl_profiling}
The uniform profiling pipeline of Chapter~\ref{ch:triton} turned
out, in retrospect, to be as valuable a contribution as the
kernels themselves. It made every number in this thesis
reproducible from a single command, and it caught several false
positives---most notably the $20\%$ regression that, after
investigation, turned out to be caused by a miskeyed auto-tune
cache entry rather than a real kernel-level effect.

\section{Limitations}\label{sec:concl_limitations}

\subsection{Single-Node Scope}\label{sec:concl_single_node}
Every measurement in this thesis is taken on a single 8-GPU
A100 node. A multi-node extension would bring an InfiniBand---or
equivalent---hop into the system, and the latency characteristics
of that hop are fundamentally different from those of NVLink-3.
Strategies aimed at reducing cross-node traffic (pipeline
parallelism, 2-D sharding, gradient compression) would therefore
very likely tilt the recommendation away from pure ZeRO-3.

\subsection{A100 Focus}\label{sec:concl_a100}
The kernel-level numbers reported here are specific to the A100.
H100 introduces asynchronous WGMMA, the TMA copy engine, DSMEM,
and an FP8 transformer engine, every one of which moves the knee
of the roofline and would demand retuning. The CUTLASS and
Triton code paths are structurally ready for Hopper; what
actually requires updating is the auto-tune search space and the
tile shapes, not the pipeline itself.

\subsection{Prototype-Level SWFA
Kernel}\label{sec:concl_swfa_proto}
The SWFA kernel is implemented in PyTorch with CPU-optimised
paths. A production-grade CUDA/Triton kernel is sketched in
Chapter~\ref{ch:triton} but has not yet been deployed in an
end-to-end serving run. The $50$--$59\%$ training speedup should
therefore be read as a lower bound---a hand-tuned CUTLASS port
can be expected to push it further.

\section{Future Work}\label{sec:concl_future}

\subsection{Short term (6--12 months)}\label{sec:concl_short}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Finish the CUDA/Triton port of SWFA and its backward
          pass, targeting a further $2$--$3\times$ speedup by
          improving Tensor-Core utilisation. Broaden the block
          classifier so that the same kernel skeleton can handle
          user-declared sparse patterns---strided, global,
          BigBird-like---beyond the sliding window.
    \item Complete the Triton auto-tuner campaign on H100 and
          publish a shape-indexed configuration cache that covers
          the most common LLM shapes out of the box.
    \item Wire SWFA into SGLang's prefill kernel, closing the
          decoupling between the prefill and decode attention
          paths.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Medium term (1--2 years)}\label{sec:concl_medium}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Multi-vendor support: port the stack to Intel Xe GPUs
          through oneDNN and SYCL, and to AMD through ROCm and
          Composable Kernel. The CuTe layout algebra is the
          portable core of the design; the vendor-specific parts
          are the MMA instruction traits.
    \item Hierarchical communication for multi-node training,
          incorporating gradient compression and error
          compensation. A plausible first step is to integrate
          Zero++ with InfiniBand-aware NCCL algorithms.
    \item Automatic policy selection: a lightweight runtime that
          picks between DDP, FSDP, and ZeRO-3, and selects the
          appropriate attention kernel, based on the model shape
          and the available hardware. The scaling-laws
          literature~\cite{hoffmann2022chinchilla} suggests that
          this decision can be precomputed offline as a function
          of $(M, N, d, w, P)$ alone.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Longer term}\label{sec:concl_long}
\begin{itemize}
    \item Co-design of the attention pattern together with the
          training curriculum. The cost model in
          Chapter~\ref{ch:swfa} assumes a fixed attention decay
          $\lambda$, but in practice $\lambda$ drifts as training
          proceeds; a schedule that widens $w$ as $\lambda$
          shrinks would turn $w^\star$ into a time-varying
          curriculum.
    \item Integration with emerging low-precision formats such
          as FP4 and NVFP6, which require fused
          scale/dequantisation epilogues. Both CUTLASS and
          Triton already expose the necessary fused instructions
          on Hopper.
    \item Carbon-aware scheduling of training runs: combining
          the energy-per-epoch numbers of
          Chapter~\ref{ch:distributed} with a real-time
          grid-carbon signal would make it possible to shift
          training windows towards low-carbon hours without
          extending the overall wall-clock of the campaign.
\end{itemize}

\section{Closing Remarks}\label{sec:concl_closing}
Efficient training and serving of LLMs cannot be reduced to a
single algorithmic trick. It requires the distributed schedule,
the kernel implementation, and the attention algorithm to be
tuned simultaneously. The work presented in the preceding
chapters treated each of these layers on its own and then showed
that they compose into substantial gains in throughput, memory
use, and energy efficiency---without sacrificing numerical
faithfulness. As models continue to grow, this holistic
perspective, rather than a succession of point optimisations
inside a single framework, is likely to remain the right way of
extracting practical performance from the hardware that is
actually available.

The author's principal hope is that the pieces assembled in this
thesis---above all the SWFA algorithm with its proof of
correctness, the controlled distributed-training comparison, and
the Triton profiling pipeline---prove useful as building blocks
well beyond the specific workloads evaluated here. The full
algorithmic pseudocode and reference PyTorch listings are
collected in Appendix~A, and the rigorous proofs that support
the main theoretical results are in Appendix~B.
